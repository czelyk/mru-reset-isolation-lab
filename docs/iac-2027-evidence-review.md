# IAC 2027 evidence review

**Current selection decision: hold.** The repository supports bounded verification on one flight-derived manager with controlled workloads. The tested configurations do not yet establish a transferable design result. This internal assessment is pending an independent skeptical review and the author's own reproduction and interpretation.

The subsequent [live receiver experiment](fprime-live-freshness.md) confirms a two-cycle benefit for delayed fault manifestation with shallow rollback on two payload calculations. It also shows no usable delivery after deeper rollback at the same producer and manager rate. A [latest-sample restart](fprime-latest-sample.md) restored usable delivery in all 36 main runs, against 12 of 36 for checkpoint replay. It skipped three or four unaccepted inputs. Preserved isolation gave a two-cycle gain in the two phase-test pairs matched at an even selected cycle. Both payloads use the same manager schedule. For replaceable inputs, the evidence favors advancing the cursor to address stale delivery. Continuing as a research candidate requires a documented consumer for which skipped samples have a measurable cost or a result that transfers beyond this schedule.

## Spacecraft relevance and closest prior work

Reset recovery during a mission-critical event and containment of fault effects are documented spacecraft engineering concerns. The NASA handbook provides representative requirements from different NASA organizations. These establish context for the experiment. They do not specify the laboratory's 100 ms period or establish a PRETTY mission deadline. [NASA Software Engineering Handbook, Fault Detection and Response](https://swehb.nasa.gov/spaces/SWEHBVC/pages/140640564/Fault%2BDetection%2Band%2BResponse).

The strongest comparison is Mru Flight's own F Prime deployment. Its operations report records separate Linux replica processes, a real manager kill after a retirement was saved, and a restore that kept the faulty replica retired. The newer fault-model note already treats known-answer testing, replica recovery and fixed wrong or missing replies. The checked-in lab extends that software path with a deliberately interrupted retirement save, a cycle-matched membership comparison and a separately scored result stream. The upstream white paper DOI was inaccessible during this review. [OPS-SAT operations report](https://github.com/mruspace/flight/blob/main/docs/opssat.md) · [Mru fault model](https://github.com/mruspace/flight/blob/main/docs/fault-model.md) · [White paper DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23305299).

Fuchs et al. already implement spacecraft-oriented checkpoint consistency with sibling checksums, recovery and a hardware reconfiguration path. Their methods distinguish the validity of state at checkpoints from what can appear on an I/O interface between them. Rollback-recovery literature also formalizes the output-commit problem. Treat stable output commit or a recipient's existing idempotency contract as competent alternatives wherever the spacecraft function supports them. The lab's one-stream SQLite watermark measures only a much narrower contract. [Fuchs et al., *Dynamic Fault Tolerance Through Resource Pooling*, sections V–VIII](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.09493) · [Elnozahy et al., *A Survey of Rollback-Recovery Protocols*, sections 2.3 and 4](https://www.cs.rice.edu/~dbj/pubs/csur-rollback.pdf).

## Strongest objection

The observed availability difference depends on when the fault becomes visible relative to the recipient's retained history and which cycles the surviving replica can serve. The experiment generator supplies a single deterministic hash workload and one ordered sequence of accepted IDs. A reviewer could reasonably describe the result as a consequence of those inputs and of Mru's existing comparison and known-answer paths.

- With a fault first applied at cycle 200, committed retirement moved fresh delivery from cycle 202 to cycle 200. The recipient's watermark removed every repeated ID in both cases. [Cycle-matched replay](fprime-matched-replay.md).
- With a replica-side fixed reply already present at restart, ordinary recovery retired that slot at cycle 190. The first correct recorder result moved from 192 to 190 with retained retirement, but every recipient first accepted a fresh correct result at 200. The stimulus was external to the restarting Linux manager. [External fault experiment](fprime-external-replica-fault.md).
- The one-tick boot check moved the recorder's first correct result earlier in the visible-fault case. It delayed fresh recipient delivery by roughly one tick in both cases with delivery, and the blind fault stopped fresh delivery in both variants. [Boot revalidation experiment](fprime-boot-revalidation.md).

The smallest remaining decision test is an online consumer with a documented interface, using an independent monotonic clock and a predefined acceptance rule. Use public source to justify what a late and a repeated sample mean for that consumer. Hold the checkpoint cycle fixed, then vary how far the recipient has advanced beyond that cycle and whether the fault is already active at restart or begins just before fresh delivery. Exercise both initial cycle parities. Compare ordinary recovery with a persisted consumer ID, a stable output-commit baseline compatible with that interface, and restored isolation. Capture the first fresh correct output at the receiver, withheld rows and wrong or repeated actions. Repeat with one workload that does not share the existing hash task or its even-cycle schedule. If the result only tracks Mru's existing cadence, keep it as a regression case for Mru. Independent hardware or stimulus and a recorder across reset domains would be needed for a board-level claim.
